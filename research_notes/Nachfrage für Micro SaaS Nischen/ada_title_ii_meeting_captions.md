## Q1. Legal status as of Oct 2026: DOJ Title II web/mobile rule (28 CFR part 35, subpart H) and compliance dates

### Takeaway
On 2026-04-20, DOJ issued an Interim Final Rule (IFR) that moved both compliance dates back by one year. Entities with 50,000+ population now have until **April 26, 2027**. Entities under 50,000 and **all special district governments** have until **April 26, 2028**. The standard is still WCAG 2.1 AA. DOJ says it will look again at whether the rule can be made "less costly". The National Federation of the Blind (NFB) has sued under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA) to vacate the delay. As of mid-October 2026 the suit appears to be pending with no ruling. Dates could move back to the original ones if NFB wins, or the content could be weakened by a later rulemaking. ⚖ lawyer.

### Cited Findings
**Original 2024 rule**
- The final rule was published in April 2024 (Federal Register document 2024-07758). It adopts WCAG 2.1 Level AA for web content and mobile apps of state and local governments. Original dates: **April 24, 2026** for public entities with 50,000+ population and **April 26, 2027** for entities under 50,000 and special districts. Sources: [ada.gov fact sheet](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/) (seen via search snippet); [UC Berkeley DAP summary](https://dap.berkeley.edu/ada-title-ii-updates/ada-title-ii-updates-what-does-it-mean-us); document number 2024-07758 as cited in [search-result summary referencing federalregister.gov](https://www.ada.gov/assets/pdfs/web-guidance.pdf).

## Q2. What the rule requires for meeting videos (WCAG 1.2.x), how the exceptions apply, whether auto-captions are enough, and enforcement

### Takeaway
After an entity's compliance date, any **newly posted** recording of a council or board meeting on its website, or on its **own** YouTube or Facebook channel, must meet WCAG 2.1 AA:
- prerecorded captions (1.2.2),
- **live captions for livestreams** (1.2.4),
- audio description where visual information is not spoken (1.2.5).

The archived-content and pre-compliance social-media exceptions protect **older** recordings only. Even then, a request from an individual still triggers effective-communication duties. Neither DOJ nor WCAG sets a numeric accuracy threshold. W3C guidance and several analyses say **unedited auto-captions are generally not enough**. No source found in this session gives DOJ an explicit "YouTube auto-captions = compliant" position. ⚖ lawyer.

### Cited Findings
- The rule requires WCAG 2.1 AA. A slide deck summarizing it says it "includes captioning requirements for live programming and audio description for prerecorded programming". Source: [ACM West slide deck "Accessibility and Captioning" (Michael Bradley), Mar 2025](https://acmwest.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Accessibility-and-Captioning-Michael-Bradley.pdf). ACM West is a community-media (local cable access) association, which suggests PEG/cable access stations are paying attention.
- WCAG 2.1 SC 1.2.2 (Level A) text: "Captions are provided for all prerecorded audio content in synchronized media, except when the media is a media alternative for text and is clearly labeled as such." Quoted in vendor analyses, e.g. [Digital Nirvana](https://digital-nirvana.com/blog/ada-title-ii-video-captioning-compliance/) and [ViddyScribe](https://viddyscribe.com/blog/ada-title-ii-requirements). Canonical text: [W3C WCAG 2.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG21/) (not fetched this session).
- SC 1.2.4 (AA) requires captions for **live** audio in synchronized media. DOJ leaves entities flexibility in how they caption. Source: [UVM WCAG rules page](https://www.uvm.edu/accessibility-services/wcag-rules-and-regulations) (search summary).
- **Accuracy:** a search summary of vendor analyses reported that DOJ **declined to set an accuracy percentage**, because captioning technology changes fast and a fixed figure could go out of date. "99%+" figures circulating online are vendor guidance, not rule text. Sources in that result set: [BoxCast](https://www.boxcast.com/blog/how-to-comply-with-ada-title-ii-captioning-requirements), [Ai-Media](https://www.ai-media.tv/knowledge-hub/insights/ada-title-ii-goes-digital-what-public-entities-need-to-know/), [Switchboard Live](https://switchboard.live/blog/captioning-for-civic-broadcasts-what-actually-counts-as-compliant), [ENCO](https://www.enco.com/use-cases/ada-title-ii-captioning-compliance-government). The exact attribution to a single page is unclear. **Check against the 2024 preamble.**
- **Vendor claims about auto-captions** (not legal authority):
  - "auto-generated captions are not sufficient unless reviewed and corrected for accuracy";
  - "unedited automatic captions are not, by themselves, a sufficient accommodation for live public proceedings under Title II".
  
  Both come from the same vendor result set: [Switchboard Live](https://switchboard.live/blog/captioning-for-civic-broadcasts-what-actually-counts-as-compliant), [Digital Nirvana](https://digital-nirvana.com/blog/ada-title-ii-video-captioning-compliance/), [BoxCast](https://www.boxcast.com/blog/how-to-comply-with-ada-title-ii-captioning-requirements). The result summary itself flagged that neither claim cites the preamble.
- **Archived-content exception:** all four conditions must be met:
  1. created before the compliance date,
  2. kept only for reference, research or recordkeeping,
  3. not altered after archiving,
  4. stored in a clearly labeled archive area.
  
  A DOJ-sourced example says city council meeting **minutes created after the compliance date do not qualify even if posted in an "archive" section**. The same logic applies to recordings. Sources: [accessible.org "How to Archive Web Content"](https://accessible.org/how-to-archive-web-content-ada-title-ii/); [ada.gov fact sheet](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/) (via snippet); [AccessAbility Officer](https://accessabilityofficer.com/blog/the-archived-content-exemption-under-title-ii-what-it-covers-and-what-it-doesnt/); [Kris Rivenburgh](https://krisrivenburgh.com/web-content-exceptions-ada-title-ii-accessibility/).
- Even content that falls under an exception must be made accessible **on request**. The DOJ fact sheet example: a city can add captions to a video and share a captioned copy with the person who asked. Sources: [ada.gov fact sheet](https://www.ada.gov/resources/2024-03-08-web-rule/) (via snippet); [UW System "Exceptions to Title II"](https://www.wisconsin.edu/digital-accessibility/exceptions-to-title-ii/).
- **Social media:** posts made **before** the compliance date are exempt. Posts after it must conform, and entities must use the accessibility features the platform offers. An entity is not responsible for the accessibility of a platform it does not itself provide as part of its services. Sources: [NC State OIT, 2024-05-15](https://oit.ncsu.edu/2024/05/15/new-ada-ruling-addresses-website-and-mobile-accessibility/); [UC Berkeley DAP](https://dap.berkeley.edu/ada-title-ii-updates/ada-title-ii-updates-what-does-it-mean-us); [Florida League of Cities deck, Kris Rivenburgh, Apr 2026](https://www.flcities.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/ADA-Web-Compliance_Kris-Rivenburgh-1.pdf).
- **YouTube precedent:** in the DOJ matter with UC Berkeley, the university's free online content on YouTube, Apple Podcasts and BerkeleyX lacked captions and other accessibility features, which shows that YouTube-hosted entity content is in scope. Source: search summary citing [UC Berkeley DAP](https://dap.berkeley.edu/ada-title-ii-updates/ada-title-ii-updates-what-does-it-mean-us).
- Official DOJ resources found but not read: the [DOJ 2022 web guidance PDF](https://www.ada.gov/assets/pdfs/web-guidance.pdf), the [Small Entity Compliance Guide](https://www.ada.gov/resources/small-entity-compliance-guide/), and the "first steps" page at https://www.ada.gov/resources/web-rule-first-steps/. That last link appears in the [ACM West deck](https://acmwest.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Accessibility-and-Captioning-Michael-Bradley.pdf).

### Inferences
- [Schätzung] **Practical scope for a meeting-caption product:**
  - Every meeting video posted after the entity's compliance date needs accurate captions (1.2.2).
  - Livestreams need live captions (1.2.4). This is technically harder and a different product from post-hoc captions.
  - Slide-heavy presentations may need audio description or narration (1.2.5).
  
  A post-meeting "upload → corrected captions (.srt/.vtt) + transcript within X hours" product covers 1.2.2. Live captioning is a separate, harder market where ENCO, Verbit, Ai-Media and Switchboard are already active.
- [Schätzung] Recordings posted to the entity's **own** YouTube or Facebook channel are the entity's own content. The third-party-content exception covers content posted *by the public*, not by the entity. ⚖ lawyer.
- [Schätzung] The value proposition ("accurate names and local terms, human-reviewed or customer-reviewed") fits the accuracy gap: no numeric standard exists, W3C and vendors say raw auto-captions fall short, and the main failure mode is proper nouns. A cheap "AI draft + clerk glossary + 10-minute review UI" product sits between free YouTube auto-captions and $1–3/min human captioning (competitor prices unverified, see Q5).
- The rule's "minimal impact" compliance provision (§35.205) may mean that minor caption errors do not automatically count as noncompliance. That is a legal interpretation (⚖ lawyer) and was not checked this session.

## Q8. Comparable rules abroad (UK, Canada, Australia): captions for meeting recordings?

### Takeaway
**Not researched this session.** Only unverified leads are below.

### Cited Findings
- None verified this session.

### Inferences
- [Schätzung] Other English-speaking markets seem to offer weaker regulatory pull for *meeting* captions than the US Title II rule. Their exemptions, as recalled below, cover live media (UK) and live captions (Ontario).

### Gaps
- [unverifiziert – Modellwissen] **UK** Public Sector Bodies (Websites and Mobile Applications) (No. 2) Accessibility Regulations 2018 (SI 2018/952): require WCAG 2.1 AA (via EN 301 549) for public sector bodies, including local councils. Regulation 4 reportedly exempts **prerecorded time-based media published before 23 Sept 2020** and **live time-based media**. Recordings of livestreamed council meetings published afterwards may need captions if kept online. Check at https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2018/952 and GOV.UK guidance.
- [unverifiziert – Modellwissen] **Ontario AODA**: the Integrated Accessibility Standards Regulation (O. Reg. 191/11, s.14) requires WCAG 2.0 AA for designated public-sector organizations, which include municipalities, **excluding SC 1.2.4 (live captions) and 1.2.5 (audio description)**. Prerecorded captions (1.2.2) therefore apply. The **federal Accessible Canada Act** covers federally regulated entities and generally **not** municipalities. Check at ontario.ca/laws/regulation/110191.
- [unverifiziert – Modellwissen] **Australia**: the Disability Discrimination Act 1992 is complaint-based. Government web standards reference WCAG 2.x AA (Digital Service Standard). There is no known specific mandate for council-meeting captions. Some states require councils to livestream meetings, e.g. Victoria's governance rules, but this was not checked.
