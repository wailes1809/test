## 1. Legal obligations and penalties (England unless stated)

### Takeaway
A 1–10 property landlord in England juggles at least six recurring or event-triggered compliance items (gas annually, EICR 5-yearly, EPC with minimum rating, deposit within 30 days, Right to Rent before letting, smoke/CO alarms at each tenancy) with penalties ranging from £5,000 (alarms) to £40,000 (electrical safety, licensing, RRA offences) and unlimited fines/imprisonment (gas, via HSE prosecution). Since 1 May 2026 most council civil penalties sit in a new two-tier regime: up to £7,000 (breach) / up to £40,000 (offence).

### Cited Findings

**Gas safety (Gas Safety (Installation and Use) Regulations 1998, enforced by HSE)**
- Landlord must arrange a gas safety check every 12 months by a Gas Safe registered engineer — [HSE: Gas safety check records](https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/landlords/gassaferecord.htm); [HSE domestic gas FAQs](https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/domestic/faqs.htm) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- The Gas Safety (Installation and Use) (Amendment) Regulations 2018 allow the check to be done any time from 10 to 12 calendar months after the previous one while keeping the original expiry date ("MOT-style" window) — [HSE domestic gas FAQs](https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/domestic/faqs.htm) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Copy of the record to existing tenants within 28 days of the check; to new tenants before they move in — [HSE: Gas safety check records](https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/landlords/gassaferecord.htm) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Records must be kept at least two years / until two further checks have been carried out — [HSE: Gas safety check records](https://www.hse.gov.uk/gas/landlords/gassaferecord.htm) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Penalty: HSE's landlord guide INDG285 states offences "could result in you facing imprisonment or a fine of up to £20 000, or both, for each offence", and in Crown Court imprisonment and/or an unlimited fine — [HSE INDG285 PDF](https://books.hse.gov.uk/gempdf/indg285.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-15). **Caveat:** this guide appears to predate current sentencing limits; the £20,000 magistrates' cap is likely outdated (magistrates' fine caps were generally lifted in 2015 — [unverified background]).

**Electrical safety / EICR (Electrical Safety Standards in the Private Rented Sector (England) Regulations 2020)**
- Fixed electrical installations must be inspected and tested by a qualified person at least every 5 years — [legislation.gov.uk SI 2020](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukdsi/2020/9780111191934); [GOV.UK electrical safety guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- **Penalty conflict:** the 2020 regulations set a maximum financial penalty of £30,000 per breach (many council pages still say £30,000, e.g. [Wiltshire Council](https://www.wiltshire.gov.uk/article/10431/19-0-The-Electrical-Safety-Standards-in-the-Private-Rented-Sector-England-Regulations-2020)); the current GOV.UK guidance says councils "may impose a financial penalty of up to £40,000" — [GOV.UK electrical safety guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- The £40,000 figure is reconciled by the new statutory civil-penalty guidance: breaches of Regulation 3, 3B, 3C and 3D carry "a statutory maximum of £40,000"; offences committed from 1 May 2026 fall under the Renters' Rights Act 2025 civil penalty regime, earlier ones under the Housing and Planning Act 2016 regime — [GOV.UK: Civil penalties under the Renters' Rights Act 2025 and other housing legislation](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/civil-penalties-under-the-renters-rights-act-2025-and-other-housing-legislation/civil-penalties-under-the-renters-rights-act-2025-and-other-housing-legislation); [GOV.UK electrical safety guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance) (accessed 2026-10-15). → **The user brief's "fines up to £30,000" is out of date for breaches from 1 May 2026; use "up to £40,000".**
- The 2020 regulations were updated in 2025 to include the social rented sector — [GOV.UK electrical safety guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance/electrical-safety-standards-in-the-private-and-social-rented-sectors-guidance) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Process: notice of intent first; landlord has 28 days for written representations; appeal to First-tier Tribunal (Property Chamber) — same GOV.UK guidance (accessed 2026-10-15).

**EPC / Minimum Energy Efficiency Standards (MEES)**
- Current minimum remains EPC E, with a £3,500 spending cap (secondary guide citing GOV.UK) — [epccertificate.uk landlord EPC rules](https://epccertificate.uk/landlord-epc-rules-2030) (accessed 2026-10-15; secondary source).
- **EPC C by 2030 is now confirmed policy, not just a proposal:** the government response, published 21 January 2026 as part of the Warm Homes Plan, confirms privately rented homes in England and Wales must reach EPC C by 1 October 2030; final rules still require legislation — [Simmons & Simmons](https://www.simmons-simmons.com/en/publications/cmkws6657001itp58cb0i2cse/the-warm-homes-plan-mees-for-privately-rented-homes-epc-c-by-2030); [CLA](https://www.cla.org.uk/news/government-announces-plan-for-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards-in-the-private-rented-sector-prs-mees/); [home-energy-model.co.uk, 2026-02-14](https://home-energy-model.co.uk/news/2026-02-14-mees-epc-c-2030-confirmed/) (accessed 2026-10-15; law-firm/trade summaries, not the GOV.UK response itself).
- Cost cap £10,000 per property over 10 years (down from £15,000 proposed), or 10% of property value if lower; qualifying spend counts from 1 October 2025; reasonable EPC assessment costs count; exemption for 10 years if cap reached; maximum penalty rises to £30,000 per property per breach; homes rated C+ before 1 October 2029 may be treated as compliant until that EPC expires; no forced heating-system change — same sources (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Original consultation (Feb 2025) context — [Elmhurst Energy, 2025-02-07](https://www.elmhurstenergy.co.uk/blog/2025/02/07/epc-c-by-2030-government-consults-on-big-changes-for-private-rented-sector/).
- Single-source claim: reformed (Home Energy Model-based) EPCs delayed to the second half of 2027 — [home-energy-model.co.uk](https://home-energy-model.co.uk/news/2026-02-14-mees-epc-c-2030-confirmed/) (unverified against GOV.UK).
- Sources conflict on short-term lets: one says scope expands to whole HMOs and short-term lets; CLA says PRS MEES will not extend to short-term lets — [CLA](https://www.cla.org.uk/news/government-announces-plan-for-minimum-energy-efficiency-standards-in-the-private-rented-sector-prs-mees/).

**Tenancy deposit protection (Housing Act 2004 ss.213–214, England & Wales)**
- Landlord must comply with the scheme's initial requirements and give prescribed information within 30 days of receiving the deposit — [Housing Act 2004 s.213](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/34/section/213) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Court must order payment of not less than the deposit amount and not more than three times the deposit (1–3x; originally fixed at 3x, made discretionary by the Localism Act 2011) — [Housing Act 2004 s.214](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2004/34/section/214); [Localism Act 2011 explanatory notes](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2011/20/notes/division/5/7/6/4) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Late compliance is not a full defence; claims possible after tenancy ends — [Sheffield City Council](https://www.sheffield.gov.uk/housing/housing-associations-tenants-advice/tenancy-law-rights-advice-tenants/tenants-unprotected-deposit) (accessed 2026-10-15).

**Right to Rent (Immigration Act 2014; England only [unverified background])**
- Maximum civil penalties (raised from 13 Feb 2024; previously £80/£1,000 first, £500/£3,000 repeat): first breach £5,000 per lodger / £10,000 per occupier; repeat breach within 3 years £10,000 per lodger / £20,000 per occupier — [GOV.UK equality impact assessment](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/digital-only-right-to-work-and-rent-checks-equality-impact-assessment/increasing-the-maximum-civil-penalty-amount-for-the-right-to-work-and-right-to-rent-schemes-equality-impact-assessment-accessible); [Code of practice 13 Feb 2024](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-rent-landlords-code-of-practice/code-of-practice-on-right-to-rent-right-to-rent-scheme-for-landlords-and-their-agents-13-february-2024-accessible-version) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Same amounts in the **latest guidance effective 1 October 2026** — [Landlord's guide to right to rent checks: 1 October 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/landlords-guide-to-right-to-rent-checks/landlords-guide-to-right-to-rent-checks-1-october-2026-accessible); [Code of practice 1 October 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/right-to-rent-landlords-code-of-practice/code-of-practice-for-landlords-and-their-agents-the-right-to-rent-scheme-for-landlords-and-their-agents-1-october-2026) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Penalties are per disqualified person; Fast Payment Option = 30% reduction if paid within 21 days, first penalty only, no instalments — same 1 Oct 2026 guide (accessed 2026-10-15).

**Smoke and carbon monoxide alarms (Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Alarm (England) Regulations 2015, amended 2022)**
- From 1 October 2022: smoke alarm on every storey used as living accommodation (now also social landlords); CO alarm in any room with a fixed combustion appliance (excluding gas cookers); repair/replace faulty alarms as soon as reasonably practicable once notified — [GOV.UK Q&A guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-alarms-explanatory-booklet-for-landlords/the-smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-alarm-england-regulations-2015-qa-booklet-for-the-private-rented-sector-landlords-and-tenants); [Explanatory memorandum SI 2022/707](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2022/707/pdfs/uksiem_20220707_en.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Enforcement: council serves remedial notice; failure to comply → penalty charge up to £5,000, applied per breach — [GOV.UK Q&A guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-alarms-explanatory-booklet-for-landlords/the-smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-alarm-england-regulations-2015-qa-booklet-for-the-private-rented-sector-landlords-and-tenants); local practice varies, e.g. West Lancashire £1,000 first / £5,000 subsequent — [West Lancashire BC](https://www.westlancs.gov.uk/housing/private-housing/help-advice-for-private-sector-tenants/smoke-carbon-monoxide-regulations.aspx) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Regulations do not apply to properties under mandatory or selective licensing (covered by licence conditions instead) — [Hull City Council PDF](https://www.hull.gov.uk/downloads/file/4242/smoke-and-carbon-monoxide-alarm-regulations-2022) (accessed 2026-10-15).

**HMO / selective licensing and the new civil penalty regime**
- From 1 May 2026, statutory guidance sets two ceilings: up to £7,000 where non-compliance is a "breach" (no prosecution route), up to £40,000 (or prosecution) for "offences"; repeat breach within five years, or continuing a breach >28 days after a penalty, escalates to the £40,000 level; no statutory minimum — [GOV.UK civil penalties guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/civil-penalties-under-the-renters-rights-act-2025-and-other-housing-legislation/civil-penalties-under-the-renters-rights-act-2025-and-other-housing-legislation) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Licence-condition breaches carry a £40,000 maximum in at least one council's 2026 policy — [East Cambridgeshire policy PDF (2026-03)](https://eastcambs.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2026-03/Agenda%20Item%2011%20Housing%20Enforcement%20Policy%20and%20Financial%20Civil%20Penalties%20Policies%202026%20-%20Appendix%202-compressed_2.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Tenants can reclaim up to 2 years' rent via rent repayment orders for certain offences (council fact sheet summary) — [Mid Suffolk RRA enforcement fact sheet](https://midsuffolk.gov.uk/documents/d/asset-library-54706/renters-rights-enforcement-fact-sheet) (accessed 2026-10-15).

**Compliance baseline (EPLS 2024, self-reported)**
- 79% of landlords reported carrying out Right to Rent checks; 91% gave prospective tenants an EPC (search-excerpt summary of EPLS 2024) — [GOV.UK EPLS 2024 main report](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/english-private-landlord-survey-2024-main-report) (published 5 Dec 2024; accessed 2026-10-15).

### Inferences
- Deadline types a tracker must model: (a) **fixed-cycle renewals** — gas (12 months, with 10–12-month early window that preserves the expiry date), EICR (≤5 years), EPC (validity period + rating threshold), PRS database (annual £65 renewal per property, see §2); (b) **event-triggered deadlines** — deposit 30 days from receipt, gas record to tenant 28 days after check / before move-in, Right to Rent before tenancy start and follow-up checks for time-limited status, alarm checks at tenancy start; (c) **regulatory horizon dates** — EPC C 1 Oct 2030, ombudsman 2028, Decent Homes 2035.
- The 2018 gas "10–12 month window" is a specific, explainable feature ("book any time in this window without losing months") that generic calendar reminders don't handle well.
- Penalty headline for marketing (sourced): "up to £40,000" (electrical / RRA offences), "up to £20,000 per occupier" (Right to Rent repeat), "1–3x the deposit", "unlimited fine/imprisonment" (gas, Crown Court).
- With section 21 abolished (§2), the old lever "unprotected deposit / missing gas cert blocks a s21 notice" is no longer the main consequence in England; the consequence shifts to fines, RROs and (for the database) blocked possession claims. ## 2. Renters' Rights Act 2025: Royal Assent, 2025–2027 timeline, devolved differences

### Takeaway
The Renters' Rights Act received Royal Assent on **27 October 2025**. Phase 1 (new periodic tenancy regime, section 21 abolished) took effect **1 May 2026**. Phase 2 starts **15 December 2026** with the "Register your rental property" PRS database (£65 per property per year, region-by-region deadlines running to **14 November 2027**), followed by mandatory Landlord Ombudsman membership in **2028**. Decent Homes Standard for the PRS is reported as **2035**; Awaab's Law for PRS still awaits consultation. As of 2026-10-15, the database date/fee is widely reported but the official GOV.UK page could not be read directly.

### Cited Findings
**Royal Assent & Phase 1 (in force)**
- Royal Assent 27 October 2025 — [House of Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/renters-reform-in-england-whats-happening-and-when/); [GOV.UK news: Historic Renters' Rights Act becomes law](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/historic-renters-rights-act-becomes-law) (accessed 2026-10-15).
- Phase 1 from 1 May 2026: new tenancy regime for new **and existing** tenancies; fixed terms end; section 21 abolished — [GOV.UK implementation roadmap (Nov 2025)](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/renters-rights-act-2025-implementation-roadmap/implementing-the-renters-rights-act-2025-our-roadmap-for-reforming-the-private-rented-sector); [PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6915beb8bc34c86ce4e6e730/Implementing_Renters_Rights_Act_2025_-_roadmap.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-15).

- Also from 1 May 2026: rules on rent increases, rent in advance, rental bidding, pets, discrimination, stronger council enforcement; landlords had to give existing tenants the government information sheet by 31 May 2026 — [NRLA key dates](https://www.nrla.org.uk/news/renters-rights-act-2025-key-dates-for-landlords); [Landlord Studio timeline](https://www.landlordstudio.com/uk-blog/renters-rights-act-implementation-timeline) (search excerpt, accessed 2026-10-15).

- Penalties: first/minor non-compliance civil penalty up to £7,000; serious/repeat up to £40,000 or prosecution; councils can for the first time issue civil penalties for illegal eviction — [GOV.UK Guide to the Renters' Rights Act](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act) (accessed 2026-10-15).

### Inferences
- **Timing window is favourable:** launching Oct–Dec 2026 lands right before the 15 Dec 2026 database opening and a rolling 11-month wave of regional deadlines (Mar–Nov 2027). Each region's deadline is a natural content/outreach hook ("West Midlands landlords: 14 March 2027").
- The PRS database adds a **new annual recurring deadline per property** (£65 renewal) and almost certainly asks for compliance info (gas/EICR/EPC) — [Inference; the database's required data fields were not verified]. This strengthens the case for a "single source of truth" tracker.
- England-only MVP is the rational scope; devolved nations add separate rule sets.

## 3. Target market size (landlords in England/UK, portfolio distribution, self-managing share)

### Takeaway
Most English landlords are small: EPLS 2024 finds 86% of individual landlords own 1–4 properties and 48% own just one. Only 18% of landlords use agent *management* services and 43% use agent *letting* services; single-property landlords are least likely to use agents (30% for letting). A reliable, sourced total landlord count was not retrieved in this session.

### Cited Findings
- EPLS 2024 (published 5 Dec 2024; fieldwork before the July 2024 election; sample = landlords/agents with deposits in the three government-backed schemes): 86% of individual landlords own 1–4 properties; 48% own one rental property — [GOV.UK EPLS 2024 main report](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/english-private-landlord-survey-2024-main-report/english-private-landlord-survey-2024-main-report) (accessed 2026-10-15). ### Inferences
- [Schätzung] Roughly 80%+ of English landlords do not use an agent for day-to-day management (100% − 18% using management services) — they personally own the compliance burden; these are the core buyers.
- [Schätzung] If England has ~2.3–2.8 million private landlords (order of magnitude often quoted; **not verified this session**), then landlords with 1–10 properties who self-manage would be on the order of 1.5–2 million. Even 0.01% conversion = 150–200 customers. Treat as order-of-magnitude only.
- EPLS samples landlords via deposit schemes, so landlords not protecting deposits (the least compliant, arguably the most in need) are under-represented.
- The PRS database itself will, by Nov 2027, produce an official count of registered landlords/properties — a future sizing source.
